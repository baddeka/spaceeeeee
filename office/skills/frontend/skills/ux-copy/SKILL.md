# Write the words

Words are part of the design. A page with real, specific text looks finished; the same page with filler looks like a template.

## Never
- Lorem ipsum, "Title here", "Lorem feature", "John Doe", "Click here", "Submit", "Welcome to our website".
- A headline that would fit any product: "Unlock your potential", "The future of X", "Simple. Powerful. Fast."
- An exclamation mark to add energy, or an emoji to add warmth.

## Headline and lead
The headline says what the visitor gets, in their words, in under ten of them. The line under it says how, or for whom. Take both from the brief: the product, its user and the problem it solves.

## Buttons and links
A verb and its object: "Draw a ticket", "Save the list", "Add a subject". The label says what happens next. The same action has the same words everywhere.

## Labels, hints, placeholders
A label names the thing ("Subject name"). A hint under it says the rule ("One question per line"). A placeholder shows an example ("Calculus"), never the label itself.

## Empty states
Say what will appear here and how to make it appear, with the action right there: "No subjects yet. Add the one you are studying for."

## Errors
What happened, and what to do, in plain words: "Write the subject's name" beats "Invalid input". Never blame the visitor, never show a code alone.

## Stand-in content
Lists, tables and cards need believable content from the product's world: real-sounding names, plausible numbers with units, dates that make sense. Vary the lengths: one long title, one short, so the layout is tested.

## Voice
Pick one from the mood in `DESIGN.md` (calm, playful, exact) and keep it on every screen. Short sentences. The visitor's language, including for dates and numbers. Write in the language the user asked for.

Read every screen once as a visitor who has never seen it: if a word can go, cut it.
